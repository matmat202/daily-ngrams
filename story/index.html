Epilogue · Reading the counts

Count one yourself

On Stanford Daily Ngrams, chart any word, or two against each other, and tap a period to read those articles in the archive.

Data: the Stanford Daily archive’s own search, the count the page uses, for every print year, fetched . A count is articles that use the word at least once, so an article saying “he” forty times counts once. Old issues are OCR scans, so early counts are low for every word. The singulars never get there: “woman” peaked at of “man”, in , partly because “man” has other senses. The web years after 2014 are left out: that search counts differently, and a ratio across the change would measure the change. This counts words, not why they changed.
